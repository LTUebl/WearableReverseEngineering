Discussion Prompts to Answer:
  1. Choose one of the design frameworks below that you find relevant to your engineering profession. Create a short discussion post describing how the framework may help with engineering design projects.
  2. Briefly describe the Wearable Technology article you read and what you found interesting about this advancement.

1. Human-Centered Design (IDEO)

Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test. Prioritizes patient comfort, ergonomic wearability, and non-stigmatizing form factors.

2. Double Diamond Design Process

Divergent & convergent problem solving: Discover (broad inquiry) → Define (focus) → Develop (ideate solutions) → Deliver (prototype & test).

3. FDA Medical Device Design Controls

21 CFR 820.30: User Needs ↔ Design Inputs ↔ Design Outputs ↔ Medical Device. Enforces Design Verification & Validation (V&V).

4. Lean Hardware Prototyping

Build – Measure – Learn fast feedback loops. Iterative breadboarding, battery power profiling, BLE telemetry, and firmware debugging.

5 Reverse Engineering Protocol Steps

1

Check Functional Operation

Check that your device is functional prior to disassembling. Record display response, button actuation, and battery health.

2

Document Disassembly & Joint Interfaces

Document the step-by-step disassembly sequence and record how each component was connected (snap fits, screws, zebra elastomeric strips, solder pads, ultrasonic welds).

3

Produce Bill of Materials (BOM)

Produce a comprehensive list of all components and count of similar components using actual engineering names (e.g., cantilever pendulum hammer, hairspring, FR4 PCB, raw segment LCD glass, elastomeric zebra strip, coin-cell terminal).

4

Component Function, Cost Estimation & Suppliers

Describe each component's engineering function, estimate component costs at 10,000-unit scale ($0.65–$1.15 typical COGS), and identify potential commercial suppliers or manufacturing processes (injection molding, metal stamping, COB wire-bonding).

5

Produce System Process Diagram

Produce a process diagram for how the pedometer and selected electromechanical components work together: from kinetic acceleration → pendulum deflection → switch contact → debounce logic → counter register → LCD multiplexer display.

Creative Ideation · Studio Pedagogy

Design Thinking & SCAMPER GenAI Ideation

Based on the curriculum by Prof. John Peponis and Dr. Eric Meyer. From Need Finding and Zero Fidelity Prototyping to AI-accelerated divergent brainstorming.

1. Need Finding & Statement Formula

Need finding discovers what a target population values and bridges the gap between goals and daily experience. Students formulate need statements adhering to:

"[Client/Population] needs [a type of solution] which [conforms to design constraints] in order to [further goal/provide value]."
Example: "People with carpal tunnel syndrome need a treatment option that is effective, easy to use, and pleasurable to alleviate symptoms and avoid surgery."

2. Zero Fidelity Prototyping (ZFP) & Bisociation

Students pair up (Partner #1 and #2) to spark radical concept exploration through Bisociation (combining an unexpected physical gesture with a character persona):

  • Brainstorming: Partner #1 poses a gesture/persona: "I could imagine..."
  • Need Finding: Partner #2 discovers the application: "That could be good for..."
  • Divergent exploration uncovers unconstrained form factors before jumping into CAD.

3. SCAMPER for Brainstorming

SCAMPER is an ideation technique for "Turning All The Knobs" on a design concept to eliminate "first idea syndrome":

Substitute Combine Adapt Modify Put to Another Use Eliminate Reverse

4. Concept Selection Matrix

Brainstorming yields a high volume of divergent concepts. Systematic evaluation requires objective selection criteria:

  • Screen out insufficient concepts against core constraints.
  • Construct a weighted design matrix (Cost, Strength, Weight, Ergonomics).
  • Balance quantitative matrix scores with engineering intuition and user feedback.

Product Selection & Research Guidelines:

To practice making and sharing these documents you will identify a wearable technology device that is interesting and that you would like to learn about and share with the class.

  • Search online for the product's webpage, reviews, teardowns, etc., such as this example teardown video: YouTube Wearable Teardown.
  • Include firsthand knowledge if you own the device or know someone who does.
  • A good source for wearable device teardowns is to search the Adafruit channel: Adafruit Wearable Teardowns Search.
  • Use AI to help with your benchmarking search for related products and summary of consumer responses.
  • Your presentation should be well organized and informative with links between the main page and specific feature pages.
  • Specific details can usually be found in the product's "Tech Specs" pages, but other information might require digging into other sources or your own reflections and experience.
  • Use multimedia (pictures, diagrams, short videos, etc.) generously, and try to limit the text on each slide.
  • Keep in mind your audience (other students) and organize the material to their level of knowledge and understanding. Double-check professional spelling and grammar.

Required 10-Slide Interactive Structure:

Slide 1 Title Slide Product name, imagery, author name, and course details.
Slide 2 Clickable Diagram (Menu) Interactive navigation menu with clickable links to all feature slides.
Slide 3 Value Proposition Mission statement, target demographic, problems solved, and user value.
Slide 4 User Inputs & Outputs Buttons, capacitive touch, haptic vibration, RGB LEDs, sensors, and feedback.
Slide 5 Mobile App Connectivity iOS/Android app pairing, BLE wireless telemetry, alert triggers, cloud data.
Slide 6 Retail & Business Strategy Retail pricing, sizing, market positioning, lifecycle, and commercial outcome.
Slide 7 Hardware Components Rigid/flexible PCB, MCU, battery, antennas, sensors, and charging case.
Slide 8 Fabrication & Assembly Folded flex PCB inside cavity, housing press-fit, charging contact pins.
Slide 9 Your Personal Review Engineering strengths, ergonomics, competitive alternatives, and weaknesses.
Slide 10 References & Sources Links to teardown videos, technical spec sheets, product reviews, and articles.
